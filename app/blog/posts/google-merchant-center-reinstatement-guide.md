# Google Merchant Center suspended? A step-by-step reinstatement guide for Shopify

A Merchant Center suspension feels like an emergency — your ads stop, your free listings vanish, and the clock on a "request review" cooldown starts ticking. Don't request a review yet. Do this first.

## Step 0: Read the suspension reason (but don't trust it to be specific)

Open Merchant Center and note the stated reason — usually **"Misrepresentation"**, **"Suspended due to policy violation"**, or a specific item-level issue. The reason is real, but Google deliberately keeps it vague. Treat it as a *category to investigate*, not a checklist.

## Step 1: Understand the cooldown — and why you only get clean shots

Here's what trips up most merchants: **the review button is disabled while you're suspended**, and **each failed re-review can lengthen the cooldown**. So firing off an appeal after fixing one thing is the worst move — you burn a review, get rejected, and wait longer next time.

The winning strategy is to fix **everything plausible in one pass**, then request a single, clean review.

## Step 2: Audit your store against what Google checks

Work through these, top to bottom:

- **Refund/return policy** — present, detailed, and linked in the footer. (Missing/unclear refund policy is the most common misrepresentation trigger.)
- **Contact info** — legal business name, email, phone, and ideally a physical address, consistent across your store *and* your Merchant Center settings.
- **HTTPS + site functionality** — secure checkout, no broken links, no placeholder text.
- **Pricing** — no fake "compare-at" prices, no undisclosed fees, feed price matches the landing page and structured data.
- **Product data** — brand + GTIN/MPN, clean images (no promo text/watermarks), real descriptions.
- **Trust signals** — no fake countdown timers or "X people viewing" widgets; visible payment badges help.

## Step 3: Fix the root causes (not the symptoms)

For each issue above, make the fix *real and durable*. Examples:
- Don't just create a refund page — link it in the footer so it's reachable everywhere.
- Don't just add an email — make sure the business name and address are consistent across the site and Merchant Center.
- Don't hide an out-of-stock product — keep the page live with the price visible.

## Step 4: Request the review — once, cleanly

Once your store genuinely addresses the categories above, request the review from inside Merchant Center. Be patient: reviews can take several business days, and a rushed, half-fixed appeal just resets the clock.

## Step 5: If you're rejected again

If a clean, thorough appeal is still rejected, the issue is almost always something you can't see from your side — an identity inconsistency, a structured-data mismatch on product pages, or a trust gap. That's where an outside, policy-grade audit pays for itself.
